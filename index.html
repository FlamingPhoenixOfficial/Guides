Guide 1 · Minecraft 1.7.10 · AE2 rv2–rv3

Applied Energistics 2, the classic way

Start with an empty world and finish with a working ME network that stores your items and crafts for you. Written for the 1.7.10 version of the mod, used by packs like FTB Infinity Evolved and SkyFactory 2.5.

  • 19 chapters
  • 4 addon sub-guides
  • About 45 minutes
  • … steps you can tick
  • Plain language

On this page

Chapter 1

What this guide teaches you

This guide teaches you the mod Applied Energistics 2 (people say “AE2”) for Minecraft 1.7.10. It starts from nothing and ends with a working ME network. Your network will store your items and craft things for you.

Everything is broken into small steps. Every step is one action. There are pictures for the tricky parts.

Before you start, you need

  • Minecraft 1.7.10 with Forge installed.
  • The mod Applied Energistics 2 (version rv2 or rv3) in your mods folder.
  • We also suggest the mod Not Enough Items (NEI). Packs can change recipes. NEI shows the real recipes in your pack. Hover an item and press R to see its recipe.

One honest warning

Different modpacks change AE2 recipes. So a recipe in this guide might not match your pack exactly. When that happens, trust NEI, not this guide. The ideas and the machines stay the same — only the recipes move.

Chapter 2

Words we use in this guide

Here are the big words, in plain language. You do not need to memorise them. You can come back here any time.

AE2
Short for Applied Energistics 2, the mod this guide is about.
ME
Short for “Matter Energy”. It is the name of the mod’s storage system. People say “ME network”.
Network
All your AE2 machines and cables connected together. They share items and power.
Channel
Think of a channel as a seat on a bus. Every machine needs one seat. A small network has 8 seats. If all seats are taken, new machines switch off until you add more seats.
Certus Quartz
A pale blue crystal you mine. It is the main ingredient of the mod.
Charged Certus Quartz
A Certus Quartz with electricity in it. It crackles with sparks.
Fluix Crystal
A purple crystal you make in water. It is used for cables and most machines.
Press
A metal stamp found in meteors. The Inscriber machine uses it to print circuits. There are 4 different presses.
Inscriber
A machine that presses circuits and builds processors (the mod’s “computer chips”).
Processor
A chip used in almost every AE2 recipe. There are 3 kinds: Logic, Calculation, and Engineering.
Storage Cell
An item that holds other items, like a digital chest. It goes inside an ME Drive or ME Chest.
Terminal
A screen where you see and take your stored items. Like a chest window, but searchable.
Pattern
A saved recipe. You teach it to the network so it can craft that item for you later.
Crafting CPU
A “brain” block structure. It remembers a big crafting job and works through the steps.

Chapter 3 · Steps 1–5

Step 1: Collect your materials

First we go shopping underground. This is everything you will need before you build anything.

  1. Dig a mine down to gold or diamond depth.

    Certus Quartz Ore lives deep underground, at about the same height as gold. Bring torches and food, like any mining trip.

  2. Mine every pale blue crystal ore you see.

    Use an iron pickaxe or better. Each ore drops Certus Quartz Crystals. A Fortune pickaxe gives you more. Keep mining until you have about 30 crystals.

  3. Watch for the sparkling ore: Charged Certus.

    It looks like Certus Ore but with tiny lightning cracks. It is rare. Take at least one if you find it. If you find none, do not worry — Chapter 5 shows you how to charge a normal crystal yourself.

  4. Take a trip to the Nether for Nether Quartz and Glowstone.

    Nether Quartz Ore is the white crystal in red netherrack. Glowstone hangs from ceilings. Bring blocks to build safe stairs.

  5. Smelt your ores and sand.

    You want iron ingots, gold ingots, and glass ready. Keep everything in one chest so the next chapters are easy.

You did it right if…

…your chest has about 30 Certus Quartz, some Nether Quartz, redstone, iron, gold, 2 diamonds, glass, and glowstone. Next: treasure hunting.

Chapter 4 · Steps 6–10

Step 2: Find a meteorite

Long ago, rocks fell from the sky. These meteorites left round holes (craters) in the world. Inside each one is a dark stone ball — and a treasure chest. The chest holds presses, and you need them to make processors. There is no other way to get them.

  1. Craft a Meteorite Compass.

    It is made from 4 iron ingots and 1 Charged Certus Quartz. Check NEI with R for the exact shape in your pack. No Charged Certus yet? Do Chapter 5 first, then come back — or just explore and look for round craters.

  2. Hold the compass and walk.

    The needle points to the nearest meteorite. If the needle spins around and around, there is no meteorite near you — keep walking and try again.

  3. Dig into the middle of the crater.

    Under the surface you will hit Sky Stone — a dark grey, almost black rock. It is harder than stone, so bring a good pickaxe. Mine some of it too, it is a nice building block.

  4. Open the Sky Stone Chest in the center.

    Inside you can find Inscriber Presses. Each meteor holds only some of them. There are 4 in total: Silicon Press, Logic Press, Calculation Press, and Engineering Press.

  5. Visit more meteors until you own all 4 presses.

    Keep them safe. They are never used up. If you play with friends, you can copy a press: put the press and iron blocks into an Inscriber. Check NEI for the exact copy recipe.

You did it right if…

…you have all 4 presses sitting safely in a chest at home. Never throw them away.

Chapter 5 · Steps 11–15

Step 3: The Charger and Charged Certus

The Charger is a small machine. You put a normal Certus Quartz Crystal in, and it comes out charged — full of electricity and crackling with sparks. You need Charged Certus for the next chapter.

  1. Craft a Charger.

    Check its recipe in NEI (hover it and press R). It is cheap: some iron and small AE2 parts.

  2. Craft a Vibration Chamber.

    This is AE2’s own generator. It burns coal, charcoal, or wood to make power. It is the easiest way to power the Charger before you have a network. If your pack has another mod with generators and power cables, those work too.

  3. Place the Charger and the Vibration Chamber touching each other.

    Power goes into the Charger through its top or bottom face. The easiest setup: Charger on the ground, Vibration Chamber directly above it. Power cables from other mods also connect to the top or bottom.

  4. Put coal or charcoal into the Vibration Chamber.

    It starts to hum and make power. A few pieces of coal are plenty.

  5. Put 4 Certus Quartz Crystals into the Charger and wait.

    Each one takes a few seconds. Out comes Charged Certus Quartz — it sparkles in your inventory. Make at least 4: you will use 1 per Fluix craft, and Fluix is your next big job.

Good habit

Whenever the Charger is running anyway, charge a few extra crystals. You will always want more Charged Certus than you think.

You did it right if…

…you have at least 4 Charged Certus Quartz Crystals. They look like Certus Quartz with tiny lightning on the icon.

Chapter 6 · Steps 16–21

Step 4: Make Fluix Crystals in water

Fluix Crystal is the purple crystal of AE2. Cables and most machines need it. You do not craft it in a crafting table — you grow it in water. This recipe is the same in almost every version of the mod.

  1. Dig a hole, 1 block wide and 1 block deep.

  2. Fill the hole with water from a bucket.

    A natural puddle or lake also works. The hole just makes it easy to pick things up.

  3. Drop 1 Charged Certus, 1 Nether Quartz, and 1 Redstone into the water.

    Select the item in your hotbar, look at the water, and press Q (the default “drop” key). All three items must float in the same water block.

  4. Watch the items bob and wiggle.

    After a few seconds, the three items pop and change. This is normal.

  5. Pick up your 2 purple Fluix Crystals.

  6. Repeat until you have about 16 Fluix.

    That is 8 rounds total. It sounds like a lot, but you will spend them fast on cables and machines.

Too slow? Use a Crystal Growth Accelerator.

This block makes things grow in water much faster. Place it so one of its faces touches the water, and give it power (your Vibration Chamber works). Not needed yet — but lovely when you batch Fluix later.

You did it right if…

…you have a stack of purple Fluix Crystals. If the items just float and never change, one of the three items is wrong — check the names carefully.

Chapter 7 · Optional steps

Optional: growing pure crystals from seeds

This chapter is optional. You can skip it and still finish the guide. But it teaches you how to never run out of crystals again.

The idea: grind a crystal into dust, mix the dust with sand to get a seed, then grow the seed in water. Out comes a pure crystal. Pure crystals work everywhere normal ones do. There are seeds for Certus Quartz, Nether Quartz, and Fluix.

  1. Craft a Quartz Grindstone and a Wooden Crank.

    The grindstone is a stone block with a hole on top. The crank is a wooden handle. Check NEI for both recipes.

  2. Place the grindstone, then place the crank on top of it.

  3. Put a crystal (or an ore) in the grindstone and right-click the crank to turn it.

    Turn it a few times until the item is ground. Crystals become dust. Ores usually give double dust — a nice bonus. Open the grindstone to take the dust out.

  4. Craft the dust with sand to make seeds.

    One dust plus one sand makes one seed — for example, Certus Quartz Dust + sand = Certus Quartz Seed. Check NEI if your pack changed it.

  5. Drop the seeds into water and wait.

    The seeds slowly become pure crystals. This is slow — about a Minecraft day. A powered Crystal Growth Accelerator touching the water makes it much faster.

  6. Fish out the pure crystals.

    Pure Certus and Pure Fluix work in every recipe that asks for the normal crystal. Your crystal supply is now renewable.

Heads up for modern players

Seeds exist only in older AE2 like 1.7.10. Newer versions grow crystals a completely different way (see Guide 2). So enjoy this little garden while you can.

Chapter 8 · Steps 22–27

Step 5: The Inscriber and processors

The Inscriber is a stamp machine. It uses your 4 presses to print circuits, and then it assembles processors — the little chips that almost every AE2 recipe needs.

  1. Craft an Inscriber and place it next to power.

    Just like the Charger, the Inscriber takes power from its top or bottom face. Put it under your Vibration Chamber, or feed it any modded power.

  2. Make Silicon.

    Grind Certus Quartz or Nether Quartz into dust (Quartz Grindstone works), then smelt the dust in a furnace to get Silicon. Some packs do it differently — if the furnace does nothing, check NEI.

  3. Print silicon: Silicon Press + Silicon.

    Put the Silicon Press in the press slot of the Inscriber and Silicon in the input slot. Out comes Printed Silicon. The press is not used up — it stays in the machine.

  4. Print a circuit: matching press + material.

    Gold with the Logic Press, Certus Quartz with the Calculation Press, Diamond with the Engineering Press. You get a printed circuit of the same name.

  5. Assemble the processor.

    Put 1 printed circuit + 1 printed silicon + 1 redstone into the Inscriber together. No press needed this time. Out comes your processor.

  6. Make a small stock: about 4 of each processor.

    You will spend them quickly on cables, terminals, and storage parts.

Lazy trick for later

Hoppers and pipes from other mods can feed the Inscriber and pull results out. Many players build one Inscriber per processor type and just leave the press inside forever.

You did it right if…

…you own Logic, Calculation, and Engineering Processors. Time to build your network.

Chapter 9 · Steps 28–32

Step 6: Your first ME network

Now the magic starts. A tiny network needs only four things: something to make power, an Energy Acceptor to feed power into the network, a cable, and your first devices — a storage box and a screen.

  1. Craft an Energy Acceptor.

    This block converts power from the outside world into energy your network understands. Check NEI for the recipe.

  2. Place the Energy Acceptor touching your power source.

    Put it next to your Vibration Chamber, or connect power cables from any other mod to it.

  3. Craft some ME Glass Cable - Fluix and place it on the Energy Acceptor.

    Cable is the road your items and energy travel on. Quilt the world in as much as you like — each piece is cheap.

  4. Craft an ME Drive and one 1k ME Storage Cell.

    The drive is a box with 10 slots for storage cells. The cell is the thing that actually holds your items. Place the drive on the cable, then right-click it and put the cell inside.

  5. Craft an ME Terminal and place it on the cable.

    Right-click the terminal. A window opens — it is empty now. Put some items in through the window (like putting them in a chest). They are stored inside the cell, in the drive. You can also search by typing.

Is the line “ME Chest” simpler?

The ME Chest is a drive and a tiny screen in one block: it holds exactly 1 cell and shows what is inside when you right-click it. Nice for a first test. Most players outgrow it quickly and use a drive plus terminal, so we teach that way.

You did it right if…

…you can open the terminal, put items in, take them out, and they stay there. If the terminal is dark, the network has no power — check the Vibration Chamber’s fuel first.

Chapter 10 · Steps 33–35

Channels, made simple

Channels scare people. They should not. Here is the whole idea in three rules:

  • Rule 1. Every device (terminal, drive, bus, machine…) uses 1 channel. Think of it as a seat on a bus.
  • Rule 2. A small network with no controller has 8 seats in total. Device number 9 will not turn on.
  • Rule 3. An ME Controller adds seats: up to 32 per side of the controller block. Big networks need controllers.

  1. Count your devices right now.

    Drive (1) + terminal (1) = 2 channels. You have 6 seats spare in your small network.

  2. Remember the magic words: “Device Missing Channel”.

    If a machine ever says that, it means the bus is full. Then it is time for an ME Controller. The controller is a later-game craft (it needs processors and lots of Fluix — check NEI).

  3. Know your cable sizes.

    Normal cables carry 8 channels. Dense cables carry 32 and are used for the busy lines out of a controller. Smart cables glow with colored lines so you can see how many channels are in use. When you outgrow 8, the usual shape is: controller → dense cable → small normal cables to your machines.

Some packs turn channels off

Server admins and packs can disable channels in the config. If your 9th machine works fine, channels are off in your game — lucky you, skip this chapter.

Chapter 11 · Steps 36–40

Step 7: Storing lots of items

Storage cells are digital chests. Two ideas matter, and both are easy:

  • Bytes are “how much stuff” a cell holds. Cells come in 1k, 4k, 16k, and 64k sizes. Bigger number, more stuff.
  • Types are “how many different items” a cell holds. Every item cell holds 63 different types — for example cobble, dirt, and torches are 3 types. Sixty-three types is a lot for normal gameplay.

  1. Craft more storage cells when you need space.

    Each cell is two crafts: a Storage Component (the expensive, processor-heavy part) and an empty Cell Housing (the cheap shell). Craft them together into a cell. Check NEI for both recipes.

  2. Fill your ME Drive with cells.

    One drive holds 10 cells. Ten 4k cells is a very comfortable home for a single player.

  3. Attach a Storage Bus to a normal chest to “adopt” it.

    Put a chest next to your cable, then place a Storage Bus on the side of the chest. Everything inside the chest now shows up in your terminal, and the network can use it. This is wonderful for huge cheap bulk storage (think: a wall of barrels of cobblestone).

  4. Learn the word “priority”.

    Every storage has a priority number. The network fills the highest number first. Give your big barrels a high priority for cobble and dirt, and your cells stay clean for nice things.

  5. Optional: partition a cell with the Cell Workbench.

    Put a cell in the workbench and set which items are allowed in it. A “cobblestone only” cell next to your quarry keeps junk out of your good cells.

The IO Port

The IO Port shuffles items between cells and the network. Its best trick: quickly empty a full cell into your bigger cells. You will want one eventually — file it under “later”.

You did it right if…

…your terminal shows everything: cell contents and the chest behind the storage bus, all in one list. Next stop: the network crafts for you.

Chapter 12 · Steps 41–48

Step 8: Autocrafting — the network crafts for you

This is the famous part. You teach the network a recipe once, and then you can ask for that item any time — it crafts it while you do something fun. Three new friends make this work:

  • ME Pattern Terminal — a terminal that also writes recipes onto Blank Patterns.
  • ME Interface + Molecular Assembler — the interface remembers patterns (up to 9). The assembler next to it is the pair of hands that does the actual crafting.
  • Crafting CPU — a box-shaped structure that thinks through big jobs step by step.

  1. Craft some Blank Patterns.

    They are cheap, empty recipe cards. Make a stack — you will use many over time. Check NEI for the recipe.

  2. Build your Crafting CPU box.

    The CPU must be a complete hollow box (like a little room): the walls are Crafting Units, and at least one wall block is a Crafting Storage (start with the 1k one). You can also add a Crafting Monitor to watch jobs and a Co-Processing Unit to go faster. When the shape is right, the blocks visually “light up”.

  3. Upgrade your terminal to an ME Pattern Terminal.

    Place it on your cable. Open it and switch to pattern-writing mode (there is a small button to swap between “crafting items” and “writing patterns”).

  4. Teach it a simple recipe.

    Put a Blank Pattern in the pattern slot. Fill the 3×3 grid with a recipe you know (for example, Quartz Glass or an ME Glass Cable). Click the arrow to write the pattern. The blank card is now an Encoded Pattern.

  5. Place an ME Interface on your cable and put the encoded pattern inside it.

  6. Place a Molecular Assembler touching the interface.

    Side by side, face to face — any side works, as long as they touch. Assemblers touching an interface can “borrow” all of its patterns.

  7. Order a craft.

    Open your terminal. Craftable items show a little “Craft” option. Click it, pick how many you want, press start, and watch the assembler work. The result lands back in your storage.

  8. Celebrate, then teach it 5 more recipes.

    Your first autocraft is a real milestone. Good candidates next: processors, cables, quartz glass, and torches.

“But my recipe needs a machine!”

In 1.7.10, patterns made in the Pattern Terminal are crafting-table recipes. For machine work (like “furnace: smelt this”), the classic setup is an Export Bus feeding the machine and an Import Bus pulling results back — see the next chapter. Newer AE2 versions added proper machine patterns; that is Guide 2.

You did it right if…

…you clicked “Craft” and items appeared in your terminal without you touching a crafting table. You now own the heart of AE2.

Chapter 13 · Steps 49–54

More useful things to try

Small devices, big quality of life. Each one uses 1 channel. Try them in any order.

  1. Import Bus — pulls items out of a chest or machine into your network.

    Great on the output side of a furnace or quarry chest.

  2. Export Bus — pushes items from your network into a chest or machine.

    Set a filter so it only exports what you want (for example, always coal into the generator).

  3. Level Emitter — a redstone switch that watches your stock.

    “Emit redstone when iron falls below 64” lets lamps, machines, or other mods react to your storage automatically.

  4. Annihilation Plane and Formation Plane — break and place blocks for the network.

    Build an automated Certus farm, or a mining turtle-free cobbleworks. These pair wonderfully with storage buses and level emitters.

  5. Wireless — check your items from anywhere in your base.

    Craft a Wireless Access Point for your network and a charged Wireless Terminal for your pocket. Infinity Booster upgrades stretch the range to “everywhere”.

  6. Late-game toys, when you are ready.

    The Quantum Network Bridge links two far-apart networks (even across dimensions). Spatial Storage cuts out a piece of the world and stores it digitally. The Matter Condenser eats junk to make singularities. None are needed for a happy base — they are dessert.

The quiet hero: Quartz Fiber

Quartz Fiber cable carries power between networks without joining them. It is how pros power a second subnet. Remember it when your base gets big.

Chapter 14 · Sub-guide · Steps 55–59

Sub-guide: ExtraCells 2 Add-on

ExtraCells 2 is the most popular AE2 addon of the 1.7.10 era. Its two big gifts: much bigger item cells, and storing fluids in your ME network — water, lava, molten metals, all of it.

What is an “addon”?

A separate mod that plugs into AE2 and adds new blocks to it. You install it like any mod: download the version made for your Minecraft (1.7.10), drop it in the mods folder, restart the game. Addon and AE2 versions must match — read the download page.

The best parts of ExtraCells 2
ThingWhy it is great
Huge item cells (256k up to 16384k)Item cells far bigger than normal AE2 allows. For hoarders and quarry owners.
Fluid cells + ME Fluid TerminalStore liquids like items, and see them in their own searchable terminal.
Fluid Import / Export BusesMove fluids between the network and tanks, just like item buses move items.
Fluid Level EmitterRedstone when a fluid is above or below your set amount.
ME Fluid AssemblerAutocrafting that uses fluids and items together, in one machine.

  1. Install ExtraCells 2 for 1.7.10 and restart the game.

  2. Craft a 1k ME Fluid Storage Cell and an ME Fluid Terminal.

    The cell goes in your drive, same as item cells. The terminal opens like your normal one — but shows liquids.

  3. Move a fluid: put a Fluid Import Bus on a tank, or a Fluid Export Bus into one.

    Point a lava tank at your network and watch buckets of it appear in the fluid terminal.

  4. Try the Ore Dictionary Export Bus with a wildcard filter.

    Set it to a group such as logWood* and it will happily feed machines every kind of log in your storage.

  5. When you are rich, upgrade one item cell to 256k or beyond.

You did it right if…

…“Lava” or “Water” shows up in the fluid terminal with an amount, like an item would.

Chapter 15 · Sub-guide · Steps 60–63

Sub-guide: AE2 Stuff Add-on

AE2 Stuff (by bdew) is a tiny addon with four machines that sand down AE2’s rough edges. Most 1.7.10 players call it essential.

The four machines of AE2 Stuff
MachineWhat it does for you
Advanced InscriberAn Inscriber with room for whole stacks of inputs. Fill it, walk away, come back to piles of processors.
Crystal Growth ChamberGrows your seeds (Chapter 7) into pure crystals automatically — powered, no water puddles.
Pattern EncoderWrites patterns without the Pattern Terminal dance, with good NEI integration.
Wireless ConnectorLinks two far-apart spots of your ME setup without a long cable trench. Drinks power by distance.

  1. Install AE2 Stuff for 1.7.10 and restart.

  2. Craft the Pattern Encoder and encode your 5 most-used recipes.

  3. Upgrade one Inscriber to an Advanced Inscriber and stock it with stacks.

  4. Let the Crystal Growth Chamber handle Fluix seeds while you mine.

Careful with the Wireless Connector

It eats more power the further it reaches. If machines mysteriously turn off after you place one, check your generator first.

Chapter 16 · Sub-guide · Steps 64–67

Sub-guide: Wireless Crafting Terminal Add-on

Normal AE2 gives you a wireless terminal that shows your items. This addon (by p455w0rd) puts a full crafting grid in your pocket — see everything, craft anything, anywhere in range.

What the addon adds
ThingWhat it does
Wireless Crafting TerminalThe pocket item: full crafting grid + your whole ME storage, on the go.
Infinity Booster CardStretches wireless range — with enough boosters, anywhere in the dimension.
Magnet Card (in many versions)Dropped items near you get sucked straight into your ME system.

  1. Install the Wireless Crafting Terminal mod for 1.7.10 and restart.

  2. Place a Wireless Access Point on your network first (see Chapter 13).

    The addon’s terminal needs vanilla AE2’s access point to talk through.

  3. Craft the terminal, charge it, and link it.

    Charge it in your AE2 Charger like any crystal. To link, open the Access Point and place the terminal in its special slot once.

  4. Craft something from your roof, just because you can.

Solar helmets, anyone?

The terminal needs battery. Keep it topped up at the Charger, or wear charging gear from other mods in your pack.

Chapter 17 · Sub-guide · Steps 68–71

Sub-guide: Thaumic Energistics & Equivalent Energistics Add-on

Playing a magic-heavy 1.7.10 pack? Two special addons let AE2 talk to the two biggest magic systems of the era. Only install the one that matches your pack.

For Thaumcraft 4: Thaumic Energistics

Thaumcraft stores magic as essentia in warded jars. Thaumic Energistics lets your ME network see and move that essentia:

  • Essentia Storage Bus — attach it to a warded jar and the essentia inside appears in your terminals.
  • Essentia Import / Export Buses — pump essentia between the network and your Thaumcraft workspace.

For ProjectE: Equivalent Energistics

ProjectE turns items into a value called EMC. Equivalent Energistics connects the two, so your EMC behaves like storage: items you have learned can appear in your terminals and be used by autocrafting, effectively pulled out of your EMC. One energy pool, endless materials.

  1. Check which mod your pack has — Thaumcraft, ProjectE, both, or neither.

  2. Install the matching addon for 1.7.10 and restart.

  3. Thaumic users: put an Essentia Storage Bus on a full warded jar.

  4. ProjectE users: search your terminal for something you learned but never stored.

Skip this if...

…your pack has no Thaumcraft and no ProjectE. These addons do nothing useful without their partner mod.

Chapter 18

If something is not working

Find your problem below and open it. The fix is inside. Almost every AE2 problem is one of these.

My terminal is dark and does nothing.

The network has no power. Check these in order:

  • Is the Vibration Chamber (or other generator) out of fuel?
  • Is the Energy Acceptor touching the generator or a power cable?
  • Did a cable break somewhere between the acceptor and the terminal?
A machine says “Device Missing Channel”.

Your bus is full: you already used all 8 channels. Remove a device you do not need, or build an ME Controller to add more seats (Chapter 10).

The inscriber is not pressing anything.
  • Check power goes into its top or bottom face.
  • Check the right press is in the press slot for that recipe.
  • Making a processor? You need all three items: printed circuit, printed silicon, and redstone.
My items float in the water and never become Fluix.

One of the three items is wrong. It must be Charged Certus (sparkly), Nether Quartz (white), and Redstone dust — all in the same water block. Normal Certus will not work.

The terminal opens but shows nothing, even though my chest is full.
  • Terminals only show item storage: cells in a drive/chest, or inventories behind a Storage Bus. A plain chest next to a cable without a Storage Bus is invisible to the network.
  • Is the storage cell actually inside the drive? Right-click the drive to check.
I clicked “Craft” and nothing is happening.
  • Is the Molecular Assembler touching the ME Interface that holds the pattern?
  • Does the network have the ingredients? Craftable or not, it cannot make iron without iron.
  • Is the Crafting CPU a complete, closed box shape with at least one Crafting Storage?

Chapter 19

Well done — you know AE2 now

You mined Certus, raided meteors, charged crystals, grew Fluix in a puddle, printed processors, and built a network that stores and crafts. That is genuinely the hard 80% of this mod. Everything else is the same ideas, bigger.

The six ideas to keep forever

  1. Meteors hold your presses. Guard them.
  2. Fluix = charged certus + nether quartz + redstone, dropped in water.
  3. Processors = printed circuit + printed silicon + redstone, in the Inscriber.
  4. Networks need power first, then devices.
  5. Channels are seats: 8 without a controller, 32 per side with one.
  6. Autocrafting = pattern in an interface + assembler touching it + a CPU box.

Playing a newer pack instead?

Modern AE2 grows crystals from living “budding” blocks and uses Pattern Providers instead of interfaces for autocrafting. It is different enough to deserve its own guide.

Applied Energistics 2, the modern way

Chapter 1

What this guide teaches you

This guide teaches you Applied Energistics 2 (AE2) for modern Minecraft — that means versions like 1.19, 1.20, and 1.21. We go from an empty inventory to a network that stores your items and crafts for you.

Modern AE2 is friendlier than the old one, but a few big things changed. This guide assumes you know nothing. Every step is one action, and hard parts get a picture.

Before you start, you need

  • Modern Minecraft with Forge, NeoForge, or Fabric, and the matching AE2 mod installed.
  • A recipe viewer mod: JEI, EMI, or REI. Packs change recipes; these show the true ones. Hover an item, press R.
  • Newer AE2 versions also include their own in-game guide book. It is excellent. This page adds the plain-language version.

One honest warning

Modpacks can change recipes, and small AE2 updates move details around. If a recipe here does not match your game, trust JEI/EMI, not us. The ideas stay the same.

Chapter 3 · Steps 1–4

Step 1: Collect your materials

Same old mining trip, slightly different rocks. Here is the list.

Your shopping list
ItemHow manyWhere you get it
Iron ingots2 stacksMine and smelt iron ore.
Redstone dust1 stackDeep caves.

Glowstone dustA fewBreak glowstone in the Nether.

Notice what is not on the list: Certus Quartz. In modern AE2 you get most of it from a meteorite, which is Chapter 4.

  1. Go caving for iron, redstone, gold, and diamonds.

    Deep slate layers are your friend. While you are down there, keep an eye out for the mod’s ores.

  2. Grab any Certus Quartz Ore you come across.

    It is a pale blue crystal ore (there is a deepslate version too). The rare sparkling kind is Charged Certus Ore — treasure it; you can also charge normal crystals later, so it is a bonus, not a must.

  3. Visit the Nether for quartz and glowstone.

  4. Smelt everything and box it up.

    One chest with all of this makes the next chapters smooth.

Chapter 4 · Steps 5–8

Step 2: Raid a meteorite

Modern meteorites are treasure boxes: Certus crystals growing inside, living crystal blocks, and in the very center a Mysterious Cube. Break that cube and you get all 4 Inscriber Presses at once. One meteor is enough.

  1. Find a meteorite.

    Look for round craters while exploring, or make a Meteorite Compass that points to one. In newer AE2 versions you charge a normal compass in a Charger to get it; in some versions it is crafted. Check JEI to see which your pack uses.

  2. Dig into the center of the crater.

    You will hit Sky Stone, a dark grey rock. Bring a decent pickaxe.

  3. Break the Mysterious Cube in the middle.

    It drops the Silicon, Logic, Calculation, and Engineering Presses. All four, every time. Put them somewhere very safe.

  4. Harvest the crystals growing inside.

    Break any fully grown clusters (the big crystals) for Certus Quartz. The next chapter explains the living blocks — do not smash everything yet.

Do not break a “Flawless Budding Certus” block

Even with Silk Touch, it breaks into a worse version, and you can never fix that. It grows crystals forever if you leave it in place. Sneak-hover or use the F3 debug screen to check a block’s name before you swing.

You did it right if…

…you have all 4 presses and a pocket of Certus Quartz, safely home.

Chapter 5 · Steps 9–14

Step 3: Grow your own crystals (budding blocks)

This is the modern way to farm Certus. Budding Certus blocks slowly sprout buds, like amethyst geodes do. Buds grow through small stages and finish as a big cluster. Break the cluster for crystals.

  1. Learn the four tiers of budding blocks.

    From best to worst: Flawless (never wears out, but breaks if moved — leave it be), Flawed, Chipped, and Damaged. Sometimes when a bud grows, the block drops one tier. When Damaged is done, it becomes a plain Certus Quartz Block.

  2. Move lesser blocks home with Silk Touch — carefully.

    A normal pickaxe knocks a budding block down one tier when you mine it. A Silk Touch pickaxe keeps the tier — except Flawless, which always gets worse. So: silk touch the lesser ones, never touch Flawless.

  3. Set up a crystal farm wall at your base.

    Place your budding blocks in a row with space around their faces so buds have room to grow. Every face can grow a bud.

  4. Add a Crystal Growth Accelerator and power it.

    Place it touching your budding blocks. Early game you can even power it with a Wooden Crank on the end — right-click the crank to turn it. Later, wire real power in. The speed difference is enormous.

  5. Harvest only the full clusters.

    A full cluster drops 4 Certus Quartz Crystals (Fortune gives more). Breaking a small bud gives just 1 dust — only do it if you specifically need dust.

  6. Fix a worn-out block with water and charged certus.

    If a budding block dropped a tier, throw it into water together with a Charged Certus Quartz Crystal. It climbs back up one tier. You can even turn a plain Certus Quartz Block into a Damaged budding one this way. (Flawless is the only tier you can never make — it is find-only.)

You did it right if…

…a wall at your base slowly grows crystals while you do other things. You now have endless Certus.

Chapter 6 · Steps 15–19

Step 4: Charge crystals, then make Fluix

Two small jobs in one chapter. The Charger fills Certus Crystals with electricity, and water turns them into purple Fluix — the cable-and-machine crystal.

  1. Craft a Charger and place it.

  2. Give it power through the top or bottom.

    Any FE power cable or generator works. No generator yet? A Wooden Crank attached to the Charger and right-clicked by hand works in a pinch, and a Vibration Chamber (burns coal) also does the job.

  3. Charge at least 4 Certus Crystals.

  4. Drop 1 Charged Certus + 1 Nether Quartz + 1 Redstone into one water block.

    Wait a few seconds. They transform into 2 Fluix Crystals. Repeat until you have around 16 Fluix — the cables and machines you craft next are hungry for it.

  5. Speed it up (optional).

    A powered Crystal Growth Accelerator touching the water makes water transformations nearly instant. Lovely when you batch Fluix later.

You did it right if…

…you own about 16 purple Fluix Crystals and a powered Charger you can reuse whenever you want.

Chapter 7 · Steps 20–24

Step 5: The Inscriber and processors

Same idea as always: presses print circuits, the Inscriber bolts them onto silicon boards with redstone, and out come processors — the chips inside every AE2 recipe. Modern quality-of-life: the Inscriber shows you the active recipe, auto-pushes finished items into adjacent inventories, and presses are never used up.

  1. Craft an Inscriber and power it (top or bottom, like the Charger).

  2. Make Silicon: smelt Certus Quartz Dust.

    Remember: breaking small crystal buds gives dust. Smelt the dust in a furnace for Silicon. If your pack changes it, JEI knows the way.

  3. Print silicon, then print all three circuit types once.

    Silicon Press + Silicon → Printed Silicon. Logic Press + Gold, Calculation Press + Certus, Engineering Press + Diamond → the three printed circuits.

  4. Assemble processors: 1 printed circuit + 1 printed silicon + 1 redstone.

    No press needed for this final step. Out come Logic, Calculation, and Engineering Processors.

  5. Stockpile about 4 of each processor.

Automation-friendly

The modern Inscriber works lovingly with hoppers and pipes, and it can push its results into a touching inventory by itself. Three Inscribers with one press each is the classic “processor factory” setup — you will see it in Chapter 10 as an autocrafting example.

Chapter 8 · Steps 25–30

Step 6: Your first ME network

Time to go digital. A first network is four things: power, an Energy Acceptor to feed the network, a cable, and devices — a drive for storage cells and a terminal as your screen.

  1. Craft an Energy Acceptor and place it touching your generator.

    Any mod that makes FE power works: thermal generators, solar, coal generators — your pack has options. The Acceptor converts that power into network energy.

  2. Craft Fluix Glass Cables and run one from the Acceptor.

  3. Place an ME Drive on the cable, then craft a 1k ME Item Storage Cell and put it in the drive.

    Cells are two crafts: the pricey Storage Component and the cheap housing. JEI shows both.

  4. Place a Crafting Terminal on the cable and open it.

    You can see, search, insert, and pull out items — plus there is a crafting grid built in. This is your new home base.

  5. Empty your pockets into the terminal.

    Shift-click your whole inventory in. Feels brilliant every single time.

  6. Understand cells: bytes vs types.

    A cell is a box of 63 egg cartons. Types = 63 cartons (63 different items). Bytes = how full the whole box can be. A 1k cell holds several thousand of one item, or smaller amounts of up to 63 different items. Bigger cells (4k… 256k) = more bytes — still 63 types.

Keep junk out of your cells

Use a Cell Workbench to tell a cell what it may hold, or park bulk junk (cobble, dirt) in barrels behind a Storage Bus instead. Your 63 types are precious — spend them on variety, not on nine kinds of gravel.

You did it right if…

…the terminal shows everything you put in, and items stay put after you close it. If it is dark, feed the Acceptor power first.

Chapter 9 · Steps 31–33

Channels, made simple

Same three rules as the old days:

  • Rule 1. Every device uses 1 channel — one seat on the bus.
  • Rule 2. No controller = 8 seats total. Device 9 stays off.
  • Rule 3. An ME Controller gives up to 32 channels per side. Cables carry 8, Dense cables carry 32.

  1. Count your devices (2 so far — drive and terminal).

  2. Upgrade busy lines to Smart Cables so you can see usage.

    No more guessing. When the lines fill up, you will know it is controller time.

  3. Plan the shape of a big network before you need it.

    Controller in the middle, dense (smart) cables like highways, thin cables as side streets to groups of 8 devices. Controllers can also share: touching controller blocks act as one big controller with more sides.

Channels off?

Some packs disable channels in config. If device 9 works fine, enjoy the freestyle life and skip the cabling anxiety.

Chapter 10 · Steps 34–41

Step 7: Autocrafting with Pattern Providers

The modern autocrafting crew:

  • Pattern Encoding Terminal — where you write patterns (recipes) onto Blank Patterns. Two kinds: Crafting Patterns (crafting-table recipes) and Processing Patterns (inputs go to a machine, outputs come back).
  • Pattern Provider — the block that holds your finished patterns and hands out jobs. This replaces the old “interface full of patterns”.
  • Molecular Assembler — the hands. It only does crafting patterns for providers it touches.
  • Crafting CPU — the box-shaped brain that tracks big jobs. Crafting Storage (1k–256k), Crafting Units as walls, optional Co-Processing Units for parallel speed and a Crafting Monitor to watch.

  1. Craft a stack of Blank Patterns and a Pattern Encoding Terminal.

    The encoding terminal is also where you search your storage — it is your new favourite screen.

  2. Build a Crafting CPU box.

    A closed hollow box of Crafting Units, with at least one wall swapped for a Crafting Storage (1k is fine to start). Add a Crafting Monitor face if you like watching progress. The blocks visually light up when the shape is built correctly.

  3. Place a Pattern Provider on your cable.

  4. Encode a crafting pattern (try Fluix Glass Cable or Quartz Glass).

    In the encoding terminal: blank pattern in, recipe in the grid, press the encode arrow. Put the encoded pattern into the provider.

  5. Place a Molecular Assembler touching the provider, then order a craft.

    Open your terminal, find the item, click Craft, choose an amount, go. Watch the assembler bob and the result pop into storage.

  6. Encode a processing pattern for the Inscriber.

    Example: “1 Certus Quartz Crystal → 1 Printed Calculation Circuit”. Only list what gets used up — the Calculation Press lives inside the Inscriber permanently, so it is not an input. Check the processing mode button in the encoding terminal.

  7. Give the result a way home.

    Stand the provider touching the Inscriber (the provider pushes ingredients in and accepts results back), or put an Import Bus on the machine. A common gotcha: a plain chest behind a Storage Bus does not count as returning results to the network.

  8. Order a full Logic Processor from your terminal and watch the chain run.

    If you taught it printed circuits and printed silicon too, one click runs the whole triangle. That moment is why people love this mod.

Pattern Access Terminal

Once patterns live in many providers, this terminal lists them all in one screen, lets you move patterns around, and highlights providers by name. Put it on your cable early.

The modern Interface is different too

In modern AE2 the plain Interface keeps a set of items stocked in itself and can dispense them — wonderful for “always keep 8 torches in this furnace cart chest” jobs. Patterns, though, belong in providers now.

You did it right if…

…you can order both a crafting-table item and a machine-made item, and both finish without you touching anything.

Chapter 13 · Sub-guide · Steps 51–54

Sub-guide: ExtendedAE & AdvancedAE Add-on

Two addons that upgrade your autocrafting furniture. They overlap a little, so many players pick one — but they can live together. Both are for modern AE2; grab the build that matches your version.

Chapter 18

If something is not working

The greatest hits of AE2 confusion. Open yours.

My Charger or Inscriber will not run.

Both machines take power through the top or bottom face. Check the generator is actually generating (fuel? sun? crank?), then re-check which face your cable touches.

My budding block disappeared (I broke it).

Budding blocks drop a tier when mined — and Flawless drops even with Silk Touch. If you already mined it, you can repair tiers: throw the block in water with a Charged Certus Crystal to climb back up one tier at a time. Flawless itself cannot be re-crafted; leave any flawless blocks you find standing in the meteor and build there, or move them later with Spatial Storage.

“Device Missing Channel”.

You used your 8 ad-hoc channels. Remove a device or add an ME Controller, and use dense cables to spread its 32-channel faces around.

My crafting job sits at “scheduled” forever.
  • For crafting patterns: is an assembler touching the provider that holds that pattern?
  • For processing patterns: can ingredients actually leave the provider into the machine — and can results come back to the network (provider contact or import bus)?
  • Does the pattern’s output match what the machine really produces in your pack?
  • Is the provider’s blocking mode stopping it because the machine still has old items inside?
My cell filled up even though it is half empty.

You ran out of types, not bytes — 63 different item kinds is the limit. Partition cells in a Cell Workbench, and move bulk single-items (cobble, dirt) to barrels behind Storage Buses with higher priority.

The terminal is dark.

No power. Feed the Energy Acceptor (or a controller) from a live generator, and check the cable path is unbroken.

What you are making

A World of Warcraft addon is a small folder of text files that the game reads when it starts. This first addon listens for the PLAYER_LOGIN event and prints a welcome message in your chat window.

Only install addons you trust

Addons are code. Get them from sources you trust, read files before running unfamiliar code, and never share your account password or security codes with an addon author. The game restricts what addons can do, especially during combat.

  1. Close World of Warcraft before changing addon files.

    This avoids editing a file while the game is loading it.

Chapter 2

Create the addon folder

Find the game folder for the version you play. Retail and Classic use different folders. In that version’s folder, open Interface, then AddOns.

World of Warcraft/
  _retail_/
    Interface/
      AddOns/
        FirstAddon/

If you play a Classic client, use its matching game folder instead of _retail_. The exact folder name can change between Classic clients.

  1. Create a folder named FirstAddon inside the correct version’s Interface/AddOns folder.

Chapter 3

Write the addon manifest

The manifest is a text file that tells the game the addon’s name and which files to load. Save it inside FirstAddon as FirstAddon.toc. Make sure your text editor does not add .txt to the end.

## Interface: 120000
## Title: First Addon
## Notes: My first small World of Warcraft addon
## Author: Your name
FirstAddon.lua

Important: The Interface number in this example is only a sample. Replace it with the current interface number for your game client before testing or sharing the addon. You can find the number in the .toc file of a recently updated addon for the same client. It changes with game updates.

  1. Create FirstAddon.toc, add the manifest lines, and use the current Interface number.

Chapter 4

Add a small Lua script

Create FirstAddon.lua in the same folder. This code listens for the player’s login event, then writes one message to chat.

local frame = CreateFrame("Frame")

frame:RegisterEvent("PLAYER_LOGIN")
frame:SetScript("OnEvent", function()
    print("FirstAddon loaded. Hello, Azeroth!")
end)

PLAYER_LOGIN happens when the game finishes logging in your character. print writes text to the chat window. This addon does not change your character or automate gameplay.

  1. Save the Lua example as FirstAddon.lua beside the manifest.

Chapter 5

Load, test, and maintain it

Start the matching game client and open the character-select screen. Open the AddOns list. If the addon is listed, enable it. If the game says it is out of date, check the Interface number and confirm you chose the right client folder.

  1. Enable First Addon from the character-select AddOns list, then enter the game.

  2. Look for “FirstAddon loaded. Hello, Azeroth!” in chat after login.

    If it does not appear, recheck the folder name, file extensions, manifest filename, and Interface number.

Game updates can change addon APIs

When a patch arrives, check current addon documentation and update the Interface number. Use Blizzard’s UI source and current community documentation to confirm which APIs are supported. Do not build addons that automate play or bypass the game’s protected-action rules.

For reference, see the World of Warcraft API guide and Blizzard’s UI source repository. Both can change; check that examples match your client version.
